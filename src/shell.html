Calculus, Fields & the Atom

One laboratory, forty-six floors, and they are laid out in the order you should climb them: the seven sections below are the seven menus in the top bar, left to right, and every wing's prerequisites are already behind it. Start on the precalculus floor and the quadratic formula gets derived rather than recited; finish on the top floor and a black hole's microstates get counted. In between: single-variable calculus, linear algebra, differential equations, the multivariable half through to the integral theorems, and the physics all of it was invented for. Everything on screen is computed live from the actual mathematics — symbolic derivatives, adaptive quadrature, Maxwell's equations, Lorentz boosts and Schwarzschild geodesics, a full nodal circuit solver, exact wavefunctions and real coupling constants. Every theorem that equates two quantities computes both of them and prints the difference.
